# ISAC-oriented Single Input, Single Output, Dual Baseline Perception Technology Research [with MATLAB Code]

Summary Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) is a key supporting technology for next-generation wireless systems, but its actual deployment often limits by low-cost single-antenna transceivers. In this dual-base station single-input single-output (SISO) architecture, clock asynchronousness leads to random phase shifts in channel state information (CSI), which traditional multi-antenna methods cannot alleviate. This paper proposes WiDFS 3.0, a lightweight dual-base SISO sensing framework that effectively suppresses Doppler echo smearing to precisely estimate delay and Doppler shift from distorted CSI. The framework uses only a single antenna at both the transmitter and receiver end, suitable for low-complexity deployment scenarios. We introduce the Self-Reference Cross Correlation (SRCC) method for SISO random phase removal and employ time-domain beamforming to address Doppler smearing issues. The extracted unblurred delay - Doppler - time features can be robustly sensed through compact neural networks. Extensive experiments demonstrate that the parameter estimation accuracy of WiDFS 3.0 is comparable or even superior to existing multi-antenna methods, especially in terms of delay estimation. Tested in both single-target and multi-target scenarios, the framework's extracted unblurred features exhibit strong perception accuracy and generalization capabilities. For example, on the MobileViT-XXS model with only 1.3 million parameters suitable for embedded devices, WiDFS 3.0 consistently outperforms traditional features such as CSI magnitude, mirror Doppler, and multi-receiver aggregate Doppler.
Introduction Integrated Sensor and Communication (ISAC) is rapidly becoming a key supporting technology for next-generation wireless systems by integrating perception capabilities into existing communication infrastructures. Its applications span multiple domains including environmental monitoring, human-machine interaction, and medical health. As ISAC moves towards practical deployment, the SISO architecture, with its low hardware complexity due to the separation of transmitters and receivers at each end, has attracted widespread attention. It is commonly found in low-power Internet of Things nodes, smart sensors, and embedded edge platforms, yet still supports reliable communication functions. This architecture is particularly suitable for applications such as home gesture control, elderly care behavior monitoring, and energy-efficient building occupancy detection, offering a practical and scalable solution for ubiquitous perception.
Most current ISAC perception research based on CSI focuses on either SIMO or MIMO architectures, where the receiver typically employs multiple antennas. This is because in scenarios where the transmitter and receiver are not synchronized, CSI can be affected by timing offset (TO) and carrier frequency offset (CFO), leading to time-varying random phase shifts. To address this issue, existing studies have proposed compensation techniques such as cross-antenna mutual correlation (CACC) and cross-antenna signal ratio (CASR), supporting a range of perception applications including target tracking, activity recognition, and water level monitoring. The development of deep learning has also spurred the emergence of various data-driven perception frameworks, which significantly enhance the performance of various ISAC tasks through techniques such as attention mechanisms, domain adaptation, and spatiotemporal feature extraction. Meanwhile, the standardization process for communication is advancing rapidly, with the IEEE 802.11bf amendment incorporating native perception capabilities into Wi-Fi, propelling ISAC technology from research prototypes to practical deployment. However, despite the robust foundation established in multi-antenna scenarios, there is still a need to further breakthroughs in developing practical, low-complexity dual-baseline SISO systems for ISAC.
The three core challenges facing ISAC-based SISO dual-baseline perception remain:
SISO Constraints on CSI Random Phase Deletion: In multi-antenna systems, the TO and CFO are consistent across all receiving antennas due to clock asynchronousness, making methods like CACC and CASR effective in eliminating phase offsets. However, these methods cannot be applied to SISO architectures. There is limited existing research on SISO perception, with some methods relying on strong line of sight (LoS) conditions (difficult to meet in actual deployment) or high computational costs (not suitable for embedded platforms). Therefore, there is an urgent need to purify raw CSI without relying on multi-antenna diversity or ideal propagation assumptions.
Doppler Mirror Blur: The communication signal bandwidth in ISAC systems is limited (e.g., LTE system bandwidth is 1.4 MHz-20 MHz), leading to low distance resolution, and Doppler characteristics become key to capturing target motion for precise perception. However, many existing systems suffer from Doppler mirror blur - energy on both positive and negative Doppler frequencies is nearly symmetric, making it difficult to determine the true direction of target movement. This issue is particularly prominent in CACC-based systems (which inherently introduce symmetrical signal components); while CASR can avoid symmetry, it mainly applies to single target scenarios and may introduce nonlinear distortion in feature extraction. Doppler mirror problems have often been overlooked in previous studies.
Learning Robust Representations from Wireless Signals: Unlike image and language tasks, wireless perception faces unique challenges - multipath propagation, environmental changes, hardware differences, and frequency bands differ significantly, causing significant variations in received signals under different scenarios. Collecting diverse and large-scale labeled datasets is costly and challenging. Although some studies use simulated data for training, the effectiveness in real deployment remains uncertain. Directly inputting raw CSI into deep networks often results in poor generalization capabilities. Moreover, data augmentation techniques commonly used in visual tasks such as rotation and cropping lack theoretical validation in wireless signal scenarios, thus designing interpretable signal features and enhancement strategies is crucial for enhancing model generalization capabilities.
In response to the challenges mentioned above, this paper proposes WiDFS 3.0—a real-time dual-baseline perception system suitable for SISO-based ISAC tasks. The system employs a separate deployment of transmitters and receivers, each equipped with a single antenna, offering a cost-effective ISAC solution. Under the influence of clock synchronization issues, WiDFS 3.0 can precisely estimate delay, Doppler shift, and unambiguous microDoppler. Based on the extracted high-quality features, it achieves excellent perception performance on lightweight neural networks deployed on embedded platforms. Additionally, the framework is scalable to multi-antenna architectures for angle estimation and is compatible with different operating frequencies and bandwidths.
Conclusion: This paper introduces WiDFS 3.0, a practical lightweight dual-baseline perception framework tailored for SISO-based ISAC systems. The framework operates using single antennas at both the transmitter and receiver ends, while also supporting expansion to multi-antenna architectures. Key innovations include: proposing Self-Reference Cross Correlation (SRCC) technology to efficiently remove random phases from CSI; designing a delay domain beamforming pipeline to generate robust three-dimensional delay-Doppler-time representations, while retaining key motion clues and suppressing interference and ambiguity; and the extracted features can be effectively utilized by compact neural networks.
The extensive experimental results demonstrate that WiDFS 3.0 consistently outperforms traditional methods in both single and multi-target scenarios, with strong feature generalization capabilities. On the Raspberry Pi 4B edge platform, without any blurring delay and Doppler feature extraction only takes 8.5 milliseconds, demonstrating real-time processing capability; despite its single antenna architecture, its median distance estimation error (20 MHz bandwidth, no smoothing) reaches 2.05 meters, outperforming existing multi-antenna systems by 0.5-2 meters, and its Doppler mirror suppression performance is comparable to multi-antenna methods. On the Single Target Widar 3.0 dataset, the MobileViT-XXS (1.3 million parameters) model achieves an F1 score of 0.928-0.938, significantly outperforming the traditional BVP baseline (0.849-0.859); on the Multi-Target WiMANS dataset, the behavior recognition F1 score is 0.659, and the count accuracy is 0.629, surpassing the commonly used CSI amplitude baseline.
In summary, WiDFS 3.0 provides a scalable, cost-effective, and robust perception solution, paving an important step towards ISAC technology's practical deployment.
